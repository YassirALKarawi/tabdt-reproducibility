# TABDT: Trust and Age Bayesian Digital Twin

Reproducibility package for:

> Y. A. A. Al-Karawi and H. Al-Raweshidy, "Trust and Age Bayesian Digital
> Twins: Provable Predictive Maintenance under Industrial Network
> Impairments," prepared for regular submission to
> IEEE Transactions on Industrial Cyber-Physical Systems, 2026.

## Contents

- `main.tex` and `IEEEtran.cls`: manuscript source and IEEE class.
- `main.pdf`: compiled eight-page manuscript, within the current 10-page TICPS
  new-manuscript limit verified on 6 August 2026.
- `tab_dt_simulation.py`: deterministic simulator for the synthetic study.
- `results.json`: raw values generated by the synthetic simulator.
- `xjtu_validation.py`: deterministic external validation on the official
  XJTU-SY run-to-failure archive.
- `xjtu_results.json`: fold-level and aggregate external validation results.
- `XJTU_SHA256SUMS.txt`: machine-checkable hashes for the XJTU-SY code and
  regenerated outputs.
- `requirements.txt`: tested Python dependency versions.
- `figs/`: vector PDF figures and 400-dpi PNG fallbacks, generated with one
  color-blind-safe Matplotlib style. Every legend sits in a data-free
  region of its axes. File `fig8_stress` is manuscript Fig. 7 and file
  `fig7_cost` is manuscript Fig. 8.

## Reproduce

Requirements: Python 3.10 or later, NumPy, SciPy, and Matplotlib.

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 tab_dt_simulation.py
pdflatex main.tex
pdflatex main.tex
pdflatex main.tex
```

The script uses fixed seeds and common random numbers across network
conditions. It regenerates `results.json` and the synthetic study figures.

## Reproduce the external validation

Download the official `XJTU-SY_Bearing_Datasets.zip` archive from the dataset
authors' public repository and verify this SHA-256 value:

```text
3cc815649a315ac7da202980c489f33db44ca2db0317bbe3bcb9dcf415375e10
```

Then run:

```bash
python3 xjtu_validation.py \
  --archive /path/to/XJTU-SY_Bearing_Datasets.zip
```

The XJTU-SY datasets are provided by Xi'an Jiaotong University and
Changxing Sumyoung Technology Co., Ltd., are publicly available for
validating bearing prognostics algorithms, and require citing the IEEE
Transactions on Reliability paper by Wang, Lei, Li, and Li (2020), which
the manuscript cites as its XJTU-SY reference.

The command checks the archive before reading it, extracts 9,216 one-minute
records from 15 bearings, regenerates `xjtu_results.json`, and writes
`figs/fig6_xjtu.pdf` plus its 400-dpi PNG fallback. The 5.45 GB archive and
derived feature cache are intentionally excluded from this package.

## Built-in validation

- Fixed-drift first-passage time: Monte-Carlo mean 501.3 h versus the
  inverse-Gaussian value 500.0 h; standard deviation 33.6 h versus 33.5 h.
- At `p=1`, B2, B3, and TABDT are numerically identical.
- The single-channel XJTU-SY component test holds a causal tenfold
  condition-baseline health-to-RUL proxy fixed across methods and isolates the
  packet-use rule: drift coasting between arrivals plus age compensation at
  arrival, against hold-last-value baselines. The proxy is an analysis
  normalization, not an asserted per-bearing failure threshold.
- Four calibration bearings imply the predictive between-bearing variance
  factor `1 + 1/4 = 1.25`; no multiplier is fitted to interval coverage. The
  primary aggregate uses all 15 physical bearings and 40 network repetitions
  per bearing. RMSE is 52.11, 53.02, and 47.70 percentage points for B2, B3,
  and TABDT. The >=100-record rule defined the prespecified 13-bearing cohort
  in the original protocol. This cohort is retained as a long-life sensitivity
  and returns 52.16, 53.01, and 47.77 points.
  Network repetitions never count as independent physical specimens.
- Paired bearing-clustered bootstrap intervals for the B2 and B3 absolute
  reductions are 3.59 to 6.72 and 4.12 to 9.56 percentage points.
- The external nominal 90% interval covers 76.9% in the 15-bearing primary
  aggregate and 76.7% in the prespecified 13-bearing sensitivity. A between-bearing factor
  sweep from 1 to 2 changes coverage from 72.4% to 84.5%, so undercoverage is
  robust to this variance sensitivity rather than presented as field calibration.
- Theorem-1 bound-to-empirical-covariance ratio ranges from 1.000 to 1.013.
- A Gilbert-Elliott stress test (`results.json` key `burst`) holds the timely
  fraction at 0.05 while the mean outage length grows from the marginally
  geometric 20 steps to 40 and 80 steps, with truths, noises, and channel
  uniforms common across regimes. It probes the independent-delay boundary
  of the theorem empirically.
- An exact reprocessing benchmark (`results.json` key `oracle`) recomputes
  the delayed-data posterior from every arrived packet on a common 25-step
  grid. It is the exact conditional law that the forward correction
  approximates, and it coincides with the sequential filter at `p=1` by
  construction, which the script asserts.
- Sensor-specific measurement variances, deadline losses, the full covariance
  term between state and drift in RUL uncertainty, and common random numbers
  are included.
- Algorithm 1 uses a Joseph-form covariance update; Algorithm 2 solves the
  proposition's unique stationary-point equation by a safeguarded bracketed
  root method.
- If `m_t` packets arrive together, Algorithm 1 costs
  `O(n^3 + m_t n^2 + m_t log m_t)`; `E[m_t] = S P_del <= S`, while the
  finite deadline gives the explicit worst-batch bound
  `m_t <= S(a_max + 1)`.
- The manuscript includes a structured comparison of representative DT-PdM,
  intermittent-estimation, AoI, and OOSM studies.
- The 22-item bibliography follows first-citation order and contains five
  directly relevant TICPS articles, 19 IEEE journal articles overall, two
  IEEE conference papers, one standards reference from NIST, and the primary
  XJTU-SY reliability paper.
- Comparative figures use prominent purple/red/blue/green curves and capped
  95% error bars.  No quantitative chart uses ambiguous shaded regions;
  single-trajectory uncertainty is shown with paired dashed limits.
- The mathematical development explicitly derives truncated-delay moments,
  effective stale-packet variance, the inverse-Gaussian RUL density, the full
  delta-method covariance, and strict network monotonicity of the certificate.
- The RUL density is explicitly conditioned on a locally frozen drift; its
  variance and Gaussian interval are labelled as first-order approximations
  and assessed by empirical coverage rather than called exact credible intervals.
- The variance theorem now yields a distribution-free one-sided Cantelli
  statement.  The Gaussian maintenance-cost expression is separately labelled
  as a simulation-validated engineering surrogate, not a theorem-implied tail bound.
- The trust score is a proposed precision calibration with a residual-based
  deployment estimator; NIST is cited only for data-quality governance.
